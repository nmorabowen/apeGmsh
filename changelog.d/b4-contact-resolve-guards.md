### FIXED — contact resolve guards: a tie on a non-flat master is refused; sector contacts on one slave label get disjoint slave sets (#1262, #1264, slice B4-c #1628)

Two resolve-time defects in `g.constraints.contact`, both found by the piles ladder on a cylindrical pile skin. A 3-D mortar tie (`tie=True`) carries one global `outward`, which the fork uses only as a per-facet sign reference; on a curved, closed or two-sided master that pairing was silently wrong (one tie measured 3.65x too stiff against a 4-sector split). `resolve_contacts` now computes the master facet normals from the node coordinates, orients each outward from the volume its surface bounds, and refuses a tie when some pair of normals is more than 90° apart, stating the widest pair and naming the remedy: one tie per sector with `master_entities=`/`slave_entities=` and a radial `outward=` each. A flat master (either sign of outward), a gently curved one and a non-tie contact are unchanged; a slab's top and bottom under one label are refused. And when several NTS contacts share one slave label with touching masters (adjacent sectors of one body), the seam nodes of adjacent slave entities landed in two contacts and the fork added their tractions; the first declaration now keeps them, a later contact drops them with a `ContactSlaveOverlapWarning` naming both contacts and the count, and a contact left with no slave nodes is refused rather than emitted as a silent no-op. Contacts whose masters share no node (two piles against one soil label) and contacts on different slave labels are untouched. The stale `interface()` docstring that said a 3-D interface does not emit is corrected; the sibling note in `opensees/_internal/build.py` is deferred (S2 freeze).
